Information: t-SNE Plots

What is a t-SNE plot?

A t-SNE plot is a scatterplot that converts high-dimensional data onto a 2D or 3D map. It is a method for visualizing the relative distance among data points.

How do I interpret the t-SNE plot?

What matters in a t-SNE plot is the relative distance of the points. Points that are closer together are presumed to be more similar. Points that are farther apart, are less similar. There is no interpretable meaning to the actual size or orientation of the plot. Also, different t-SNE plots should not be compared for analysis, since the are all scaled relative to their own datasets.

What data does the t-SNE use?

This t-SNE plot will pull from a table of values in CODAP. The table can be built in CODAP directly, or be dropped in from an existing csv file. The table must contain at least one column of data that will be converted into vector embeddings and plotted. The table may optionally include a “label” column, that the user predicts indicate similar types of phrases. For example, if your table contained phrases about everyday actions, you might label some of them as “eating” and some as “playing.” This interactive will allow up to 4 labels.

How do I use the t-SNE plot interactive?

Choose your table. Then select which columns hold your phrases, and optionally, your own labels. The graph will plot. You can add additional phrases and see where they appear. You can also examine the plot for clusters.

How do the colors relate?

The colors are your own imported labels. You can compare how you sorted the data versus what the embeddings saw.

How does the interactive decide where to place my test phrases?

The interactive converts your phrase into an embedding vector. It then applies nearest neighbors to see where it would appear on this flattened graph.
