Information: t-SNE Plots

What is a t-SNE plot?

A t-SNE plot is a scatterplot that represents high-dimensional data in two or three dimensions. It is a method for visualizing patterns of similarity among data points.

How does the plugin convert the phrases in the dataset into points?

First, this plugin uses a process called Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) to convert text into numbers. It identifies which words are present in the dataset, assigning a value to each one based on their frequency in the phrases and overall dataset. Less frequent words receive a higher weight. Then each phrase gets a vector, or set of numbers, with one value for each identified word. Second, this plugin uses t-SNE to reduce these vectors to two coordinates for each phrase, so they can be plotted in the two-dimensional plane.

How do I interpret the t-SNE plot?

What matters in a t-SNE plot is which points appear near each other. Points that are closer together tend to be more similar. There is no interpretable meaning to the actual size or orientation of the plot. Also, t-SNE plots built from different datasets should not be directly compared for distances or positions, since each plot is generated independently.

What data does the plugin use?

This plugin will pull from a table of values in CODAP. The table can be built in CODAP directly, or be dropped in from an existing CSV file. The table must contain at least one column of text that will be converted into numerical vectors and plotted. The table may optionally include a “label” column, where you can list what you believe to be categories in the data. For example, if your table contains phrases about everyday actions, you might label some of them as “eating” and some as “playing.”

How do I use the t-SNE plot plugin?

First, choose your data table. Then select which column contains your phrases and, optionally, which column contains your own labels. The graph will appear, and you can examine the points to look for clusters or patterns. You can also add additional phrases and see where they appear.

How do the colors relate?

The colors on the graph relate to the labels provided in the dataset. If no labels are provided, all data points from the table will be gray.

How do I use the Test Phrases section?

After you have created your t-SNE plot, you can add new phrases and see where they land in the graph. Simply type your phrase into an open slot, and press Enter. If your phrase shares any recognized words with the original dataset, it will appear on the graph using the icon next to your phrase. You can change the phrase for each icon as many times as you wish.

How does the plugin decide where to place my test phrases?

The plugin converts your phrase into a vector using the vocabulary and weights determined when creating the initial plot. It then compares this vector to the ones created for the original phrases, finds the most similar one, and places the point for your phrase close to that one. If your phrase has no overlapping recognized words with the original dataset, the plugin will not plot your phrase. When this occurs, the box containing your phrase will change colors.

How can I inspect the t-SNE plot?

The plugin offers several ways for you to examine your data. If you place your cursor over a dot, hovertext will appear showing you the beginning of your phrase. If you click on a dot, the corresponding table will scroll to that row with highlighting for you to read. If you click on one of the Key labels, all the data points that correspond with that label will be highlighted both on the plot and in the table. Similarly, clicking on the icon next to your test phrase will cause that icon to be highlighted in the plot. Clicking on any row in your data table will highlight that point in the plot. You can use the + and - buttons to adjust the scaling of your plot, and then click and drag the blank spaces to move your focus. You can click on the button to refit the scaling to show the full plot.
